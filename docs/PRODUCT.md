# TouchStone — Product Definition

## One Sentence

A worry-offloading system that tells you exactly what "enough" looks like today, so you can stop planning and start trusting.

---

## The Core Problem

Most productivity apps ask: "What should you do? How can you do more?"

But for people who struggle with executive function, the problem isn't knowing what to do. The problem is:

- **Morning paralysis**: "What's most urgent? Where do I even start?"
- **Constant anxiety**: "Am I doing enough? Will I make my deadline?"
- **Guilt about rest**: "If I stop now, will everything fall apart?"
- **Decision fatigue**: "Should I work on A or B? What if I choose wrong?"

These aren't productivity problems. They're **anxiety problems** disguised as productivity problems.

---

## What TouchStone Actually Does

**TouchStone is a worry-offloading system.**

It answers the questions you carry in your head so you don't have to:

| Your Worry | TouchStone's Answer |
|------------|---------------------|
| "What should I do first?" | "I sorted it. Here's today's plan." |
| "What if I don't finish everything?" | "You don't need to. Today's plan = enough." |
| "Am I going to miss my deadline?" | "No. I calculated backward. You're on track." |
| "Should I push harder today?" | "No. The plan accounts for sustainable pace." |
| "Can I take a break?" | "Yes. Breaks are in the plan. Take them guilt-free." |
| "What if I have a bad day?" | "I'll redistribute. Your future is still safe." |

**The promise**: Give me your deadlines, your projects, your fixed events. I'll tell you exactly what "enough" looks like each day. Follow the plan, and your future is secure. Stop carrying the mental load.

---

## The Philosophy

### "Enough" Is the Goal, Not "More"

Most apps implicitly say: more is better, always optimize, never waste time.

TouchStone says: **there is a number called "enough," and when you hit it, you should stop.**

- Enough work to meet your deadlines (with buffer)
- Enough rest to stay sustainable
- Enough space for life outside of work

The app defines "enough" mathematically so you don't have to guess.

### Trust the System, Stop Planning

Every morning, instead of:
> "What should I do? Let me think about priorities, check deadlines, estimate effort, feel anxious..."

You see:
> "Here's today. 5 hours of work across 4 sessions. Deadlines safe. Gym at 6pm. Follow this and you're good."

No decision required. The system already decided. You just execute.

### Bad Days Are Built In

The system doesn't assume perfect humans. When you skip a day:

1. No judgment, no guilt messaging
2. Automatic redistribution across remaining days
3. Clear communication: "You're still on track" or "Here's the adjusted plan"

Rest days are first-class citizens, not failures to log.

### Work Has a Finish Line

When you hit your daily "enough":

- The app actively tells you to stop
- Life activities (gym, hobbies, rest) become the focus
- No more work suggestions until tomorrow

This isn't laziness. This is **sustainable by design.**

---

## Core Concepts

### Stones (Reality)

Fixed, immovable events: meetings, appointments, commitments. They represent what cannot change. The app plans around them, not through them.

### Water (Flexible Work)

Projects and tasks that flow into available time. The "liquid scheduler" pours work into gaps between stones, sized appropriately for each slot.

### Habits (Life Priorities) — *Planned*

Recurring commitments to yourself: gym 3x/week, reading time, family dinner. Auto-scheduled into available slots. Treated as seriously as work deadlines.

### "Enough" Threshold

A calculated daily target based on:
- Your deadlines and remaining work
- Your stated capacity (default: 6 productive hours)
- Buffer for unexpected things

When you hit it, you're done. Really done.

## What TouchStone Is

- A **worry-offloading system** that carries the mental load of planning
- A **daily clarity engine** that shows exactly what "enough" looks like
- A **permission-granting tool** that says "yes, you can stop now"
- A **bad-day absorber** that adapts without judgment
- A **life-protector** that schedules habits alongside work

## What TouchStone Is NOT

- Not a task manager (we don't track every little thing)
- Not a productivity maximizer (we optimize for sustainable, not maximum)
- Not a guilt machine (no overdue badges, no shame)
- Not a planning tool (we do the planning so you don't have to)
- Not a tracking tool (logging is lightweight, not comprehensive)

---

## Success Criteria

### The App Works When:

1. **User opens app without anxiety** — seeing the day feels calming, not overwhelming
2. **User trusts "enough"** — when the app says stop, they believe it
3. **User follows the plan without re-planning** — no second-guessing the schedule
4. **User takes rest days without guilt** — the system said it's okay, so it's okay
5. **User protects non-work time** — habits happen because they're scheduled

### The App Fails When:

1. User opens app and feels they need to make decisions
2. User ignores "enough" and keeps working from guilt
3. User spends time reorganizing/replanning instead of executing
4. User skips rest and feels the app judged them
5. User treats habits as "skippable" extras

## Appendix: Why This Matters for ADHD/Executive Dysfunction

TouchStone is designed with neurodivergent users in mind. Here's how the design addresses specific challenges:

| Challenge | How TouchStone Helps |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Decision paralysis** | No decisions needed — the plan is pre-made |
| **Time blindness** | Visual timeline shows exactly when things happen |
| **"Where do I start?"** | The first task is right there. Just that one. |
| **Guilt spirals** | "Enough" is defined externally, not by feelings |
| **All-or-nothing thinking** | Bad days redistribute, they don't ruin everything |
| **Difficulty stopping** | App actively tells you when to stop |
| **Forgetting self-care** | Habits auto-scheduled as real commitments |
| **Overwhelm from big projects** | AI breaks projects into 60-90 min sessions |
| **"But what if...?" anxiety** | Deadline math is visible and verifiable |

The goal isn't to "fix" executive dysfunction. It's to **externalize the functions that are hard** — planning, prioritizing, knowing when enough is enough — so the user can focus on just doing the next thing.
